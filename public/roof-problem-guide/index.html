Problem 1 of 8

Roof leak

Call today
Shows upTop-floor ceilings, attic
Often far fromThe actual hole

How to recognize it

Stains on top-floor ceilings, drips during rain, or wet insulation in the attic.

Why it matters

Water travels along rafters, so the leak inside can be feet away from the opening on the roof.

Signs to look for

  • Ceiling stains that grow after rain
  • Drips during storms
  • Wet attic insulation
  • Peeling paint near the ceiling

Do this now

  1. Bucket under the drip; move belongings.
  2. Stay out from under sagging drywall.
  3. Photograph inside and outside.

Call today. Many roofers can tarp the roof to stop more water.

Problem 2 of 8

Wind-lifted shingles

Call today
Look forMissing or lifted shingles
Also checkEdges and ridge

How to recognize it

Shingles in the yard, bare patches on the roof, or shingles lifted and creased along the edges and ridge.

Why it matters

Exposed underlayment can't keep out rain for long.

Signs to look for

  • Shingles on the ground
  • Bare or dark patches on the roof
  • Lifted or creased shingles
  • Debris or branches on the roof

Do this now

  1. Photograph the roof from the ground.
  2. Stay off the roof.
  3. Note the storm date for your records.

Call today if shingles are missing.

Problem 3 of 8

Hail hits

Call this week
ClueDents on vents, AC, mailbox
On shinglesDark bruises, lost granules

How to recognize it

Dents on soft metal like vents and the AC unit, dark spots where granules were knocked off, or cracked shingles.

Why it matters

Hail bruises can be hard to see but shorten a roof's life and can lead to leaks.

Signs to look for

  • Dented vents, flashing or AC fins
  • Granules near downspouts
  • Dark spots on shingles
  • Cracked or split shingles

Do this now

  1. Photograph dents on vents and the AC unit.
  2. Write down the storm date.
  3. Stay off the roof.

Call this week for an inspection, while the storm date is fresh.

Problem 4 of 8

Worn or aging shingles

Not urgent
Asphalt lifeAbout 20 to 30 years
SignsCurling, cracking, bald spots

How to recognize it

Shingles curling at the edges, cracking, or losing granules, with patchy, bald spots.

Why it matters

Worn shingles leak more easily and blow off in storms.

Signs to look for

  • Curling or cupped shingles
  • Granules collecting on the ground
  • Cracked shingles
  • Patchy color

Do this now

  1. Look at the roof from across the street.
  2. Check the attic for stains.
  3. Note the roof's age if you know it.

Not urgent. Plan an inspection before storm season.

Problem 5 of 8

Sagging roof

Call today
Look forDips in the roofline
CausesWater-rotted deck, heavy snow

How to recognize it

A dip or wave in the roofline, or bowed rafters and boards in the attic.

Why it matters

Sagging can mean rotted decking or structural strain. It needs a prompt look.

Signs to look for

  • Dips or waves in the roofline
  • Bowed attic boards
  • Cracks in top-floor ceilings
  • Doors sticking upstairs

Do this now

  1. Keep people out from under the area.
  2. Clear heavy snow only from the ground with a roof rake.
  3. Photograph the roofline.

Call today.

Problem 6 of 8

Flashing failure

Call this week
WhereVent pipes, roof openings, wall joints
CommonLeading cause of leaks

How to recognize it

Leaks or stains near vent pipes or where the roof meets a wall; loose, rusted or bent metal.

Why it matters

Flashing seals the joints where leaks start most often.

Signs to look for

  • Stains near vent pipes
  • Loose or rusted metal
  • Cracked sealant
  • Leaks only in driving rain

Do this now

  1. Note exactly where the stain is inside.
  2. Photograph vents and wall joints from the ground.
  3. Bucket under any drip.

Call this week; today if it's actively leaking.

Problem 7 of 8

Attic warning signs

Call this week
Look forDaylight, stains, soft boards
AlsoWet or matted insulation

How to recognize it

Daylight through the boards, dark water stains, soft or delaminating sheathing, or damp insulation.

Why it matters

The attic shows roof problems early, before they reach your ceilings.

Signs to look for

  • Daylight through boards
  • Dark stains on rafters
  • Soft or flaking sheathing
  • Damp insulation

Do this now

  1. Look with a flashlight, staying on the joists.
  2. Photograph stains and light gaps.
  3. Put a bucket under drips.

Call this week.

Problem 8 of 8

Moss and algae

Not urgent
Dark streaksUsually algae, cosmetic
MossHolds moisture, lifts shingles

How to recognize it

Black streaks on shingles, or green clumps of moss on shaded parts of the roof.

Why it matters

Streaks are mostly cosmetic. Moss traps moisture and can lift shingles over time.

Signs to look for

  • Black streaks
  • Green clumps on shaded areas
  • Shingles lifting near moss
  • Debris piling up

Do this now

  1. Don't pressure-wash shingles.
  2. Trim overhanging branches if safe.
  3. Photograph the affected areas.

Not urgent. Ask about cleaning when you schedule an inspection.

How to

Before you call

Stay off the roof

Roofs are slippery and steep. Photos from the ground or a drone are enough.

Photograph everything

Inside and out, plus dents on vents and the AC after hail.

Note the storm date

Write down when the storm hit. It helps the roofer and your records.

Catch the water

Bucket under drips, belongings moved, and stay out from under sagging drywall.

Check the attic

A flashlight look for stains, light and damp insulation tells the roofer a lot.

Know your roof's age

Age and material help the roofer plan repair or replacement.

FAQ

Roof problem questions

How do I know if my roof is leaking?+

Ceiling stains that grow after rain, drips during storms, peeling paint near the ceiling or damp attic insulation.

What do hail hits on a roof look like?+

Dark bruises where granules were knocked off, cracked shingles, and dents on vents, flashing and the AC unit.

How do I know if wind lifted my shingles?+

Shingles in the yard, bare patches on the roof, or lifted and creased shingles along the edges.

Why are there shingle granules on the ground?+

Some loss is normal on new roofs. Heavy loss on an older roof, or after hail, means the shingles are wearing out.

Is a sagging roof dangerous?+

It can be. It may mean rotted decking or structural strain. Keep people out from under it and call.

What is roof flashing?+

Metal that seals the joints around vent pipes, roof openings and walls. It's one of the most common leak points.

Can I patch a roof leak myself?+

It's safer to bucket the drip, take photos and call. Roofs are a common fall risk.

How long does an asphalt shingle roof last?+

Often about 20 to 30 years, depending on climate, ventilation and the shingle type.

Is moss on my roof a problem?+

Dark streaks are mostly cosmetic. Thick moss holds moisture and can lift shingles over time.

Should I get a roof inspection after a storm?+

Yes, especially after hail or high winds, even if you don't see a leak yet.

Can a roofer tarp my roof?+

Many do emergency tarping to stop water until a repair can be made.

Repair or replace my roof?+

A small, isolated problem on a younger roof usually means repair. Widespread wear, many leaks or a soft deck often mean replacement.
