Where roof leaks usually start

  • Flashing around vents, walls and valleys.
  • Pipe boots whose rubber seals have cracked.
  • Missing or cracked shingles after wind or hail.
  • Ice dams in cold climates, pushing meltwater under shingles.
  • Worn roofing at the end of its life, leaking in several spots.

Water travels along rafters and decking, so the stain on your ceiling is often a few feet from where the water gets in. A roofer finds the entry point, not just the stain.

Do this now

  1. Catch the water in a bucket and move valuables out of the way.
  2. If the ceiling is bulging with water, keep people out of the room.
  3. Photograph the ceiling and the roof from the ground.
  4. Call a roofer. A temporary tarp can stop more water getting in until the repair.

How the repair usually goes

The roofer inspects the roof and the attic, traces the water to where it enters, and repairs that spot: new flashing, a new pipe boot, replaced shingles or a section of new underlayment. If the roof is near the end of its life, they may suggest a re-roof instead, and you decide.

FAQ

Roof Leak Repair: common questions

Is a roof leak an emergency?

An active leak into living space should be handled as soon as possible. Call and say it's leaking now; many roofers can tarp the same day.

Why does my roof only leak in heavy rain?

Wind-driven rain reaches spots normal rain doesn't, such as flashing joints and lifted shingle edges. It's still worth fixing before it spreads.

Can a leak be fixed in winter?

Yes. Roofers repair leaks year-round and use temporary measures when it's too cold for some materials to seal.
