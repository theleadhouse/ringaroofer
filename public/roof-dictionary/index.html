ABCDEFGHIMNOPRSTUVW

A

Air sealing
Closing gaps where warm house air leaks into the attic, such as around lights, pipes and the attic hatch. Related help
Algae streaks
Dark streaks on shingles, common in humid climates; mostly a looks issue. Related help
Architectural shingles
Thicker, layered asphalt shingles with a dimensional look. Also called laminated or dimensional shingles. Related help
Asphalt shingles
The most common roofing in the U.S.: a fiberglass mat coated in asphalt and topped with granules. Related help
Attic insulation
Insulation on the attic floor that keeps house heat out of the attic, which helps prevent ice dams. Related help
Attic ventilation
Air moving in at the bottom of the roof and out near the top to remove heat and moisture. ENERGY STAR explains how it works. Related help

B

Blistering
Small bubbles on asphalt shingles that can pop and lose granules. Related help

C

Cedar shake
Split wood roofing with a rough, natural look. Related help
Clay tile
Fired clay roofing tiles, common in warm climates; long lasting but brittle underfoot. Related help
Concrete tile
Heavy molded concrete roofing tiles. Related help
Cool roof
A roof surface that reflects more sunlight and stays cooler. The Department of Energy explains the benefits in hot climates. Related help
Counter flashing
Flashing set into or over a wall that covers the top of the step flashing. Related help
Course
A single row of shingles across the roof. Related help
Cricket (saddle)
A small peaked section that sends water around something on the roof, such as behind a wall, instead of letting it pool. Related help
Curling (cupping, clawing)
Shingle edges or centers lifting as shingles age, often on sunny slopes. Related help

D

Dormer
A structure that sticks out of a sloped roof, often with its own small roof. Related help
Drip edge
Metal edging along the roof's edges that directs water away from the edge boards. Related help

E

Eaves
The bottom edges of the roof that hang past the walls. Related help
Emergency tarp
A heavy tarp secured over an opening to keep water out until a proper repair. Related help
EPDM
A black rubber membrane used on flat roofs. Related help
Exposed-fastener metal roof
A metal roof with screws through the panels, each sealed by a rubber washer. Related help
Exposure
The part of each shingle you can see once the next row is on. Related help

F

Flashing
Thin metal pieces that seal joints where the roof meets walls, vents and valleys. Related help

G

Gable
The triangular wall at the end of a pitched roof. Related help
Granules
The colored grit on asphalt shingles that protects them from sunlight. Losing lots of them is a sign of wear. Related help

H

Hail bruise
A soft spot on a shingle where hail cracked the mat underneath, sometimes with granules knocked off. Related help
Hip
The sloped line where two roof planes meet on the outside corner of a hip roof. Related help
Hose test
Running water over one roof section at a time to find exactly where a leak enters. Related help

I

Ice and water barrier
A self-sealing membrane put under shingles along the edges and valleys to resist leaks from ice dams and wind-driven rain. Related help
Ice dam
A ridge of ice at the roof edge that traps melting snow behind it. Related help
Intake vents
Vents near the bottom edge of the roof that let outside air into the attic. Related help

M

Modified bitumen
An asphalt-based flat roof membrane applied in layers. Related help
Moss
Plants that grow in shady, damp spots on roofs and hold moisture against shingles. Related help

N

Nail pop
A roofing nail that has backed out and lifted the shingle above it. Related help

O

Overlay (roof-over)
Installing new shingles over an existing layer. Local codes limit how many layers are allowed. Related help

P

Permit
Permission from your city or county for roofing work, often followed by an inspection. Related help
Pipe boot
A rubber or metal collar that seals around a plumbing vent pipe on the roof. A common leak spot as it ages. Related help
Pitch (slope)
How steep a roof is, written as rise over 12 inches of run, like 6/12. Related help
Plumbing vent stack
The pipe that sticks up through the roof to vent the house's drains; sealed with a pipe boot. Related help
Ponding
Water that sits on a flat roof for days after rain. Related help

R

Rafters and trusses
The framing that holds up the roof deck. Related help
Rake
The sloped edge of a roof at the end of a gable. Related help
Ridge
The top line where two roof slopes meet. Related help
Ridge vent
A vent along the ridge that lets warm, moist attic air out. Related help
Roof deck (decking, sheathing)
The wood boards or panels the roof is nailed to. Related help
Roof penetration
Anything that goes through the roof: vents, pipes, fans. Each one needs a good seal. Related help

S

Scupper
An opening at the edge of a flat roof that lets water drain off. Related help
Sealant strip
The adhesive line on asphalt shingles that sticks each shingle to the one below in the sun's heat. Related help
Slate
Natural stone roofing; very long lasting and heavy. Related help
Square
Roofers measure roofs in squares: one square is 100 square feet of roof. Related help
Standing seam
A metal roof with raised seams joining the panels and hidden fasteners. Related help
Starter strip
The first row of shingle material along the edges that helps seal the first course and resist wind. Related help
Step flashing
Small L-shaped flashing pieces woven in with shingles where a roof meets a side wall. Related help
Storm chaser
A term for contractors who travel to areas hit by storms and knock on doors. The FTC advises caution. Related help

T

Tapered insulation
Insulation boards cut with a slope to help a flat roof drain. Related help
Tear-off
Removing the old roofing down to the deck before installing a new roof. Related help
Three-tab shingles
Flat asphalt shingles with three tabs per strip; thinner than architectural shingles. Related help
TPO
A white single-ply membrane used on many flat and near-flat roofs. Related help

U

Underlayment
A water-resistant layer under the shingles or tiles that's a second line of defense against leaks. Related help

V

Valley
The channel where two roof slopes meet. Lots of water flows through it. Related help

W

Workmanship terms
What the installing company says it will do if its own work has a problem. Ask for it in writing. Related help
