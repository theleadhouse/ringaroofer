The short answer

  • Don't sign anything on the spot.
  • Check out any contractor before you hire.
  • Be wary of large up-front payments.

From the ground

What to check first

Stay off the roof. Everything here can be done from the ground, inside, or from a safe spot in the attic.

  1. 1

    Ask for their local address and references.

  2. 2

    Check reviews and complaints independently.

  3. 3

    Ask for a written scope of work.

  4. 4

    Ask your state or local government what credentials roofers need.

  5. 5

    Ask for photos of any problems they say they found.

Practical steps

What to do now

What you can do

  • Take their info and check them out.
  • Get a written scope.
  • Get more than one opinion.
  • Pay in stages.
  • Keep records.

What to avoid

  • Don't let anyone on the roof without checking them out.
  • Don't sign on the spot.
  • Don't pay in full up front.

What a roofer usually does

  • A reputable roofer gives you time to decide.
  • Puts the scope of work in writing.
  • Doesn't demand full payment up front.

Questions people ask

Roofer knocked on your door after a storm: 12 questions people ask

Are all door-to-door roofers scams?

No, but the FTC warns about home repair scams after storms.

How do I check a roofer?

Check references, complaints, a local address and the credentials your state requires.

Can I cancel a contract I signed at my door?

Some states have cancellation rules for door-to-door sales; check with your state consumer office.

Should I sign something on the spot?

No. Take time, read it, and get a second opinion.

What should a written agreement include?

The work, materials, timeline, cleanup, permit and payment schedule.

What if they say the offer ends today?

Pressure to decide fast is a warning sign the FTC flags.

Should I pay cash up front?

The FTC warns against paying in full up front.

What is a storm chaser?

A term for contractors who travel to storm areas and knock on doors.

Can I take time to think about an offer?

Yes. A reputable roofer will give you time.

Is it OK to let a door-to-door roofer on my roof?

Be careful. The FTC suggests checking out contractors before agreeing to anything.

What if they offer to handle everything for me?

Read the agreement closely and don't sign anything that hands over decisions you don't understand.

Can I get a second opinion?

Yes. Many homeowners do before any big roof job.

Words you may hear

Storm chaser
A term for contractors who travel to areas hit by storms and knock on doors. The FTC advises caution.
