The basics

How this part of your roof works

Metal roofs are panels held by screws or hidden clips. Many leaks come from screws whose rubber washers have aged, or from seams and flashing.

Tile roofs (clay or concrete) shed water from the tiles, but the underlayment beneath does a lot of the waterproofing. Cracked or slipped tiles let more water reach it.

Flat roofs (TPO, EPDM, modified bitumen and others) need to drain. Water that sits for days, called ponding, strains seams and the membrane.

When to call a roofer

Call when water is coming in, shingles or panels are missing, something hit the roof, or a problem keeps coming back.

FAQ

Metal, tile & flat roofs: questions people ask

Why is my metal roof leaking?

Often worn screw washers, loose screws, seams or flashing.

Can I walk on a tile roof?

It's safer not to. Tiles crack underfoot. A roofer knows where to step.

How long should water sit on a flat roof?

Many roofers say water still sitting a couple of days after rain is a problem.

Are metal roofs noisy in rain?

They can be, depending on what's underneath. Attic insulation and decking help.

Can cracked tiles be replaced one at a time?

Usually, yes, if matching tiles are available.

What's a flat roof membrane?

The waterproof sheet on top of a flat roof, such as TPO, EPDM or modified bitumen.

Words you may hear

Hip
The sloped line where two roof planes meet on the outside corner of a hip roof.
Standing seam
A metal roof with raised seams joining the panels and hidden fasteners.
Exposed-fastener metal roof
A metal roof with screws through the panels, each sealed by a rubber washer.
Clay tile
Fired clay roofing tiles, common in warm climates; long lasting but brittle underfoot.
Concrete tile
Heavy molded concrete roofing tiles.
Slate
Natural stone roofing; very long lasting and heavy.
Cedar shake
Split wood roofing with a rough, natural look.
TPO
A white single-ply membrane used on many flat and near-flat roofs.
